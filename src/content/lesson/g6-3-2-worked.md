## What this is about

Sometimes two fractions look completely different but mean **exactly the same
amount**.

$$
\frac{1}{2} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{2}{4} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{3}{6}
$$

All three of those are the same thing. They all show **half**.

Fractions that mean the same amount are called **equivalent fractions**.

> They are *equivalent* because they are **equal in value**, even though the
> numbers on top and bottom are different.

## Why this matters so much

You will not use equivalent fractions much on their own. But they unlock
everything after them:

- **Adding fractions** with different bottoms
- **Decimals**, like 0.5 and 0.50
- **Ratio** and **rate**
- **Percentage**
- And eventually **algebra**

So this is a stepping stone. Getting it properly now saves a lot later.

---

## The one thing to understand first

A fraction is really a **division question in disguise**.

$$
\frac{1}{2} \quad \text{means} \quad 1 \div 2
$$

The line in the middle, called the **fraction bar** or just the bar, means
**divide by**. Remember that. It explains almost everything.

So when we look at two fractions, the question is:

> Do these two divisions give the same answer?

$$
1 \div 2 = 0.5
$$

$$
2 \div 4 = 0.5
$$

$$
3 \div 6 = 0.5
$$

Same answer every time. **Equivalent.**

---

## How to make equivalent fractions

Here is the method. We will do it properly in the worked example below, but
here is the idea:

To turn one fraction into an equivalent fraction, you **multiply or divide the
top and the bottom by the same number**. The same number. Both of them. Always
both.

> **Why does this work?**
>
> Because multiplying the top and bottom by the same number is really
> multiplying the whole division by that number twice — once on each side. And
> any fraction multiplied top-and-bottom by the same number stays the same
> amount. It is like measuring the same length in centimetres and in
> millimetres. The length did not change. The unit did.

### Working it out

Try $\frac{1}{2}$ and make it bigger.

Multiply top and bottom by 2:

$$
\frac{1 \times 2}{2 \times 2} = \frac{2}{4}
$$

Both the 1 and the 2 got multiplied by 2. So it is still half.

### The most useful direction

Most of the time we want to **make the bottom number match** something else,
because adding fractions needs matching bottoms.

So we usually **go up**, not down:

$$
\frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{5}{10}
$$

> **Careful, and this is the most common mistake:**
> You can **never** add or subtract on the top and bottom on their own.
> Only together, and only by the same number.

## Worked example

### The problem

> Shade $\frac{2}{3}$ of this bar. Then write it again with a bottom number of 6.

### Step 1 — Understand what the fraction is asking for

The bar is split into **3 equal parts**. We need **2** of them.

$$
\frac{2}{3}
$$

**Why this step matters:** the bottom number tells you how many pieces. The top
number tells you how many to take. If the bar is not actually split into 3 equal
pieces, the fraction is not showing you the right thing. This is the single
most common way to read a diagram wrongly.

### Step 2 — Write down what we have and what we need

We have: bottom number **3**.
We need: bottom number **6**.

$$
\frac{2}{3} = \frac{?}{6}
$$

**Why:** writing both numbers down stops the most common error, which is
changing the bottom number and forgetting the top one.

### Step 3 — Work out what we multiplied the bottom by

From 3 to 6 is multiplying by **2**.

$$
6 \div 3 = 2
$$

**Why:** because we are going to multiply **both** numbers by the same thing, the
bottom number tells us what that thing is. Find the multiplier from the bottom
number, because that is the number we are matching.

### Step 4 — Multiply the top number by the same thing

The top number was 2. Multiply by 2 as well:

$$
2 \times 2 = 4
$$

**Why this is the important step:** if you got this wrong, the answer would not
be half of anything, it would be a different amount altogether. The two numbers
**must** be multiplied by the same number, or the fraction changes value.

### Step 5 — Put it together and check

$$
\frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{6}
$$

Check: is 4 out of 6 the same as 2 out of 3? Halve both: 2 and 3. Yes. Correct.

**Why check at all?** Because you can halve or double both numbers to get back to
the original. If you cannot, something went wrong. This check takes three
seconds and catches nearly every mistake.

## Going the other way

Sometimes the bottom number is too big and you need to make the fraction
**smaller**. Then you divide instead.

$$
\frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4} = \frac{1.5}{2}
$$

Or using the check above: 6 and 8 can both be divided by 2 to give 3 and 4. So
$\frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}$.

> **The same rule, both directions.** Multiply both by the same number to go up.
> Divide both by the same number to go down.

---

## The mistake to avoid

This is the one that catches everybody:

> ## ❌ Never add or subtract the top and bottom on their own.

Going from $\frac{1}{2}$ to $\frac{2}{3}$ by adding 1 to the top and 1 to the
bottom does **not** give an equivalent fraction.

$$
\frac{1}{2} \ne \frac{2}{3}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{2} = 0.5 \qquad \frac{2}{3} = 0.666\ldots
$$

**These are different amounts.** You changed the value by adding to each number
separately.

The correct move is to multiply or divide **both together by the same number**.
From $\frac{1}{2}$ to $\frac{2}{3}$ there is **no** such single number, so
**these two fractions are not equivalent and never will be.**

---

## Why some fractions cannot be made equivalent

Once you get to the bottom number being a **prime number** — 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 —
there is no way to multiply both numbers to get a matching bottom, unless you
use fractions.

That is why, in the next lesson on adding fractions, we sometimes need a
**common denominator** that is not as simple as matching the bottoms exactly.
$\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{1}{3}$ have no matching bottom. You will need 6.

You do not need to worry about that yet. Just notice: **2, 3, 5, 7 and 11 are
the hard ones.**

---

## What to remember

1. The fraction bar means **divide by**.
2. Equivalent fractions mean the **same amount**.
3. Multiply or divide **both numbers by the same number**. Never one on its own.
4. **Check** by halving or doubling back.
5. The bottom number is the **hard one** when it is 2, 3, 5, 7 or 11.
