# The Grade 6 Mathematics Syllabus

**Source: NIE Mathematics Teacher's Guide, Grade 6 (2015), nie.lk**

This is the official syllabus, taken directly from the National Institute of
Education teacher's guide. Not a summary from a website.

Grade 6 is called **Grade 6** in Sri Lanka. See the
[glossary](./glossary.md) for why "Year 7" is a UK mapping, not a local name.

---

## What this syllabus is

### The format

NIE does not just list topics. Every unit has four things:

| Part | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Competency** | What a student should be able to *do* |
| **Competency level** | The numbered sub-skill (1.1, 1.2...) |
| **Content** | The actual topic |
| **Learning outcomes** | What a student can do by the end |

Grade 6 has **19 competencies, 24 competency levels, 157 periods.**

The competency numbers are shared across Grades 6-11. Not every number exists
in every grade. Grade 6 has no competencies 5, 10, 15-20 or 25-27.

### How long a lesson is

**40 minutes per period.** The teacher's guide gives each competency level a
prescribed lesson structure with quality inputs, activities and assessment
criteria.

## What is NOT in Grade 6

This surprises almost every parent and student:

**There is no probability.** It first appears at Grade 11.

**There is no mean, mode or median.**

**There is no Pythagoras. No trigonometry.**

**There are no percentages.** (They appear at Grade 11, lesson 9.)

**There are no circle theorems.**

**There are no equations to solve.** Only algebraic *symbols* and
*substitution*.

**There is no volume or surface area of solids.** Only perimeter and area.

**There is no coordinate geometry. No transformations.**

> **Why this matters:** if a parent asks for a Grade 6 lesson on percentages, the
> honest answer is that it is not on the syllabus yet. Teaching it early is not
> the same as teaching it correctly, and it often creates the confusion that
> resurfaces in Grade 11.

## Why Grade 6 is the right place to start

### The dependency chain

Grade 6 is where the foundation is laid, and it is a chain:

```
Multiplication tables (1.6, up to 100)
        ↓
Factors and multiples, divisibility (1.6, 1.7)
        ↓
Fractions - unit, equivalent, comparing, adding (3.1-3.4)
        ↓
Decimals to 2dp (3.5, 3.6)
        ↓
Ratio and rate (4.1)
        ↓
Areas and volumes through Grade 10
        ↓
Fractions, ratio and percentage at Grade 11
```

**A student who is shaky on 1.6 cannot do 3.1 properly.** Fractions require
LCM and HCF, which require prime factorisation, which requires knowing multiples
up to 100.

**This is the gap our target student has**, and it opens here.

### The 1-period warning

Competency 3.2, equivalent fractions, gets **one 40-minute period.**

Equivalent fractions is the gateway to adding and subtracting fractions, ratio,
percentage, and eventually algebra. **One lesson for that is not enough** for a
student who is struggling.

> **This is a specific, official, defensible place for our product to help. Not
> because fractions are hard, but because the syllabus allocates them very
> little time.**

## The teaching notes NIE gives

Each competency level has prescribed materials. The Circles lesson (24.1) calls
for:

> "Demy Paper, Coloured A4 paper, gum, pairs of scissors, Circular items of
> various sizes (coins, bangles, saucers, bottle tops, circular lids)"

**The official syllabus already prescribes hands-on, physical learning.** That
matches the CRA research in
[08-teaching-method.md](./08-teaching-method.md) almost exactly.

We are not fighting the syllabus. We are trying to deliver it better on a
smaller budget.

## Curriculum context

From the Director General's message in the Grade 6 teacher's guide:

- The **8-year competency-based cycle** entered secondary education in 2007
- **Grades 6 and 10 started the rationalized (new competency-based) syllabus in
  2015**
- Grades 7, 8, 9 and 11 follow the same 2007 cycle

**Practical consequence:** the O-Level exam changed from 2017 onward. Papers
before 2017 are old syllabus. Any question bank must be labelled.

## What this means for building

### Content order should follow the syllabus

Not a made-up order. The official sequence, because:

- Teachers teach it that way, so students arrive in that order
- Prerequisites line up
- We can map every page to an official unit number

### The Grade 6 opportunity, stated precisely

25 lessons, 157 periods, one year.

That is a **manageable amount of content**. A focused team could write all of it
properly. It is small enough that doing it well is realistic.

Compare with the O-Level syllabus: 25 units, 190 periods, but covering
trigonometry, matrices, calculus-adjacent content, and proof. **Grade 6 is
where quality is achievable.**

### Vocabulary matters

Content should use the NIE terms, not invented ones. The teacher's guide gives
the vocabulary teachers use, in English, even for Sinhala and Tamil classrooms.

> A student who searches our site for the term their teacher used is far more
> likely to find the lesson than one searching for our invented phrasing.
