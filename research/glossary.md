# Glossary

Sri Lankan education has its own words. This file explains them in plain
English, so that everything else in this folder is easy to read.

## Words you will see everywhere

### OL / O-Level

**Ordinary Level.** The national exam students sit at the end of **Grade 11**.

- The main checkpoint. Whether a student can get into a university, a diploma,
  or an apprenticeship depends mostly on this result.
- **The exam month is normally December.** But it has been badly disrupted
  recently, so always check the published calendar before planning anything.
  O/L 2026 is scheduled for 8-17 December 2026. See
  [Exams and past papers](./05-exams-and-papers.md).
- Results come roughly 3-4 months after the paper. A December exam means
  results in March or April.
- We write it as "O-Level" in full, or "O/L" in short.

> **Planning tip:** because the exam date moves, never build a study schedule
> that hard-codes the exam month. Always count back from the published date.

### AL / A-Level

**Advanced Level.** The exam after O-Level, taken in **Grades 12 and 13**.

- Two years long. Students pick a stream, and maths has separate subjects:
  **Mathematics (code 07)**, **Combined Mathematics (10)**, and **Higher
  Mathematics (11)**.
- A student needs a good O-Level result to get in.

> **Pure Maths and Applied Maths are A/L, not O/L.** At O-Level there is only
> **one** Mathematics subject (code 32). We had this wrong earlier.

### Year 7

**Careful: this is a UK mapping, not a Sri Lankan name.**

Sri Lanka officially calls it **Grade 6**. Our earlier draft claimed the
official name was "Year 7" - that was wrong, and the National Education
Commission's own policy framework has to correct it:

> *"it is not in line with international nomenclature for a country following
> the UK system of education as it uses **"Grades" as opposed to "Years"** to
> indicate the duration. This is because the kindergarten year in Sri Lanka is
> designated as Grade 1... This has often created a misconception."*
> - NEC, National Education Policy Framework 2020-2030

The mapping, if you need it for international comparisons:

| UK system | Sri Lanka |
| --- | --- |
| Year 6 | Grade 5 |
| **Year 7** | **Grade 6** |
| Year 12 | Grade 11 |

In Sinhala, Grade 6 is **6 ශ්‍රේණිය** (sixth year). In Tamil it is
**ஆறாம் வகுப்பு** (sixth class). **Both simply mean "sixth."**

> **So: use "Grade 6". It is correct in every language. Never write "Year 6" for
> Grade 6 - in Sinhala and Tamil that means the sixth year, which is Grade 5.**

### Medium

The language of instruction. In Sri Lanka a child's **medium** is fixed early
and usually stays with them:

- **Sinhala Medium** — Sinhala. **73% of students.**
- **Tamil Medium** — Tamil. **24% of students.**
- **Bilingual** — mother tongue plus English. About 2.5% of students.

**O/L Maths is sat in all three languages.** The Department of Examinations
publishes every paper in Sinhala, Tamil and English. A student sits the exam in
their own medium.

> **Earlier drafts of this research said most Sinhala and Tamil students sit the
> English paper. That was wrong.** They sit the paper in their own language.

This matters for us: a maths term like "fraction" may reach a student as
*කොටස* (*kōṭhasa*), *பகுதி* (*pakuthi*), or "fraction". Content has to be able
to speak the student's language, not just show a translation.

### NIE

**National Institute of Education.** The government body that writes the
official syllabus, the resource books, and the teacher guides.

This is where the real syllabus documents live. If our content does not match
NIE, it does not match the exam.

### MoE / DoE

- **MoE** — Ministry of Education. The policy level.
- **DoE** — Department of Education. Runs schools day to day, and handles the
  O-Level exam itself.

### Paper I and Paper II

O-Level Maths is **one subject** (code 32) split into **two papers**, worth
**100 marks each, 200 in total**.

- **Paper I** - 2 hours. Part A is 25 multiple-choice questions. Part B is 5
  longer structured questions.
- **Paper II** - 3 hours, plus 10 minutes reading time.

### MCQ

**Multiple Choice Question.** One question, five options, one correct answer.

Note: at O-Level Maths, MCQs are **2 marks each**, not 1. And they are answered
on the question paper itself, not on a separate machine-readable sheet.

> **A common mistake:** students assume all of Paper I is multiple choice. It is
> not. The second half of Paper I is structured questions worth 10 marks each.

### Past papers

Real exam papers from previous years. These are the single most valuable study
resource a student can have, because they show the *actual* style and difficulty
of the questions.

### Unit

A section of the syllabus. The O-Level Maths syllabus has **25 units** across
three terms, totalling 190 teaching periods.

### Competency and Competency Level

How the NIE syllabus is written. Instead of just listing topics, each unit has:

- A **competency** - what a student should be able to *do*
  ("Manipulates the mathematical operations in the set of real numbers")
- **Competency levels** - the numbered sub-skills (1.1, 1.2, 1.3...)
- **Content** - the actual topic
- **Learning outcomes** - what a student can do by the end
- **Periods** - how many 40-minute lessons are allocated

Grade 6 has 19 competencies across 24 levels and 157 periods. Grade 11 has 25
units across 190 periods.

### Rationalized syllabus

Grades 6 and 10 were the entry points for the current competency-based
curriculum, starting 2015. The O-Level exam changed with it: papers from **2017
onward use the new syllabus**, earlier papers use the old one.

> **Any practice question bank must be labelled by syllabus vintage.** A 2015
> paper and a 2025 paper are not testing the same course.

### Z-Score

A number calculated from the **district** results. Because exam difficulty
changes each year, raw marks cannot be compared across years. The Z-score
normalises them.

Students often worry about the Z-score more than the pass mark, which surprises
people from other countries.

### Cut-off

The minimum result needed to enter a particular course. Cut-offs rise and fall
every year depending on how many students apply.

## Everyday teaching words

### Tuition

Extra classes outside school hours. Extremely common in Sri Lanka, and
sometimes expected for most subjects by the time a student reaches O-Level.

Common home-based versions: a tutor comes to your house, or a small group of
students gathers at the tutor's house.

### Sirona

Colloquial for tuition. You will hear "sirona classes" used more than
"tuition" in daily speech.

### Past paper book

A compiled book of previous years' papers. A normal part of a student's
revision setup.

### Notebook / exercise book

Students copy lessons into a notebook by hand. Many students say they
understand better when they write it out, which is worth remembering when we
design the site.

## Words about us

### Part-time tuition teacher

Our starting point. Teaching a small number of students, a few hours a week,
outside normal working hours.

### MathsGuru

The name of this project. "Guru" is used in everyday Sinhala speech to mean a
teacher or a well-respected expert in something.
