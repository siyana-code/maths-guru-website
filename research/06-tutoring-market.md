# The Tutoring Market in Sri Lanka

**Research date: 9 October 2026**

This document looks at how maths tuition actually works in Sri Lanka today, what
it costs, and what that means for our website.

---

## The most important thing to understand first

**In Sri Lanka, tuition is not an extra. It is a parallel school system.**

Education has been free since 1945. School is free. But if a student wants a
good result, tuition is how most families get there. This is not a sign of a
broken system. It is how families respond to an exam that decides everything
about a child's future.

### How big is it?

- **65% of urban households** and **61% of rural households** use private
  tutoring (IPS/UNESCO, 2016)
- Tuition is about **43% of household education spending**
- The country spends around **LKR 121-122 billion a year** on tuition, against
  LKR 402 billion on government schools

That last number is the one to remember: **parents spend roughly 30% of what
the government spends on schooling, on extra classes.**

### Who does the teaching?

Mostly **government school teachers**, tutoring on the side. A study of
Anuradhapura found that most tutors are male government teachers, and that
nearly half use their leave entitlement to teach tuition.

Students also stay home from school more often near the O/L and A/L exams,
because they are at tuition instead. Tuition has partly replaced school rather
than adding to it.

### Why should we care?

Because a website is not competing with free YouTube. It is competing with a
massive, trusted, expensive, human system that families have used for
generations.

> **Our honest position:** we are not trying to replace the tutor. We are trying
> to make the student better prepared *before* the tutor session, and better
> at practice *after* it. The tutor stays. We make the tutor's hour go further.

## What families pay

> **Warning:** there is no official price list for tuition anywhere in Sri Lanka.
> Everything below comes either from live classified adverts (asking prices, not
> agreed prices) or from self-reported salary sites we do not trust much. Treat
> these as rough, not exact.

### What families spend per month

| Household income | Tuition spending |
| --- | --- |
| Under LKR 30,000 | LKR 3,000 - 7,000 |
| Over LKR 200,000 | LKR 18,000 - 20,000 |

For context, the average household spends about **LKR 2,400 a month on all
education** including tuition, stationery and books.

### What tutors charge (from live adverts)

These are real listings we found in October 2026:

| Type of class | Advertised price |
| --- | --- |
| O/L Maths, Gr 10-11, 3 hrs/week, Colombo, in person | LKR 2,000/month |
| O/L Maths home tuition | LKR 1,500 - 3,000 |
| Online class, Grade 7 Sinhala medium | LKR 1,500/month |
| Online class, Grade 10 Sinhala medium | LKR 2,000/month |
| Combined Maths A/L (2 hrs twice weekly) | LKR 5,000 per class |
| Physics A/L, online | LKR 3,000/hour |
| London O/L, online | LKR 2,500/hour |

**The pattern is clear: O/L maths for a school student costs roughly
LKR 1,500 - 3,000 per month.** A/L and international syllabuses cost much more.

### Colombo versus countryside

We could not find proper data on this. Our best estimate, based on Colombo
advertised prices plus one rural data point:

| Area | Likely rate |
| --- | --- |
| Colombo / Western Province | LKR 1,000 - 1,500/hour |
| Small towns | LKR 400 - 800/hour |
| Deep rural / estates | Very low. Effectively nobody can make a living here |

The one hard rural data point is hard to forget: an English tutor in rural
Batticaloa charged **LKR 40 per class** in 2023, and said he kept his prices low
because he knew the students could not afford more.

### One number that matters for us

A single tutor advert on panthi.lk costs **LKR 4,990 per month**.

If a tutor earns LKR 15,000 a month from three students, they are spending about
a third of their income on advertising. That is a lot. It is why small tutors
struggle, and why so many of the directory sites look thin and half-built.

## How students actually find a tutor

**We have to be honest here: there is no published data on how Sri Lankan
families search for tutors.** Nobody has studied it. What follows is our
reasoned guess based on how the platforms are built and how big they are.

### Platform sizes in Sri Lanka

| Platform | Users |
| --- | --- |
| Facebook | 9.0 million |
| YouTube | 8.8 million |
| TikTok | 6.8 million (growing fastest, +25% a year) |
| WhatsApp | No official number, but everywhere |

### Our guess at the order

1. **Facebook** - still number one for finding a tutor. It is where parents
   are, and there are busy tutor-hunting groups. Listings usually end with a
   WhatsApp number.

2. **WhatsApp** - where the actual conversation and decision happen. Every
   single platform we looked at funnels people to WhatsApp.

3. **YouTube** - where parents do their research. A parent will watch a few
   Sinhala maths videos before paying anyone.

4. **TikTok** - growing fastest, but we have no evidence it converts to paid
   tuition.

5. **Google** - small, but almost completely unclaimed. **This matters to us.**

### The gap we can use

The current flow is:

```
Facebook post  ->  WhatsApp message  ->  cash or bank transfer
                ->  no record of anything
```

Nobody keeps track. There is no history, no progress record, no way for a
parent to see what their child actually learned.

**That is our opening.** Not to replace the tutor. To be the thing that
remembers.

## How tuition is delivered, and what works

### The options

| Method | Good | Bad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tutor comes to your house** | Highest trust. No device needed. Works without internet. | Tutor wastes 1-2 hours a day travelling. Does not scale. |
| **At the tutor's house** | Costs are shared better. Parents already trust this. | Safety worries for teenage girls. Student must travel. |
| **Small group (3-6 students)** | Best money per hour for the tutor. Friends learn from each other. | Scheduling 6 different families is a nightmare. |
| **Online live classes** | No travel. One tutor can reach students far away. | Only 21% of families have a computer. Maths on a phone screen is painful. Power cuts kill sessions. |
| **Recorded lessons** | No power, data, device or timing problems. | No live questions. |
| **Hybrid** | Recorded for theory, small group live for practice. | Needs content made in advance. |

### The real constraint: devices and data

This is the thing most online education gets wrong about Sri Lanka.

- **Only 21.3% of households own a computer or laptop.** Rural: 18.6%. Estates:
  5.2%.
- **80% of device use is on smartphones.** A student's device is a shared
  phone.
- **O/L and A/L students spend over LKR 2,000 per month on data** just for
  school and tuition. For a family earning under LKR 30,000, that is over 7% of
  their income.
- Sri Lanka had a **nationwide blackout in February 2025.**

### What this means for us

> **Design for the phone, on a slow connection, that can lose power.**
> Never assume a laptop. Never assume WiFi. Never assume the student can watch
> a video.

A few important consequences:

1. **Everything must work on a phone first.**
2. **Text and images beat video.** Video is expensive in data and useless when
   the connection drops.
3. **It must survive a power cut mid-question.**
4. **Low data cost is not a nice-to-have. It decides whether people use it.**

## How money is paid in Sri Lanka

The honest answer: **mostly cash or bank transfer.**

- **Cash is still king**, especially outside Colombo. 52% of online shoppers
  still prefer cash on delivery.
- **eZ Cash and mCash** are the mobile wallets almost everyone has.
- **LankaQR** works and is fee-free under LKR 5,000 from March 2026, but
  actual use is still low.
- **Cards are unusual** for small amounts.

### What every online tuition site actually does

Look at the two Sri Lankan platforms that process payments:

> Dreams Online: "Make a bank transfer and upload your receipt. Admin verifies
> within 24 hours."
>
> Edugo.lk: "Pay by bank transfer or cash. Upload your receipt and the teacher
> approves your access."

Same pattern. **Upload receipt, wait for a human to approve.**

That works, but it is slow and it causes arguments. If we ever handle money,
our goal should be to **remove the manual step**, not to add another payment
option.

### Our decision for now

**Do not build payment processing.** It is not our problem to solve, and we are
not selling paid classes yet.

If we ever charge for something:

- LKR price points in the 1,500 - 3,000 monthly range
- Bank transfer first (free and instant between banks now)
- eZ Cash for families without a bank account
- Never require a card

## The problems that make this hard

### Parents pay. Students use.

This is the biggest structural problem. The parent holds the money and signs
off. The student chooses whether to actually do the work.

- Parents want **results**. They cannot see them until exam day.
- Students want it to be **easy and not embarrassing**.

A product that only appeals to students gets signups and then churn. A product
that only appeals to parents gets no students.

### Trust is everything

The entire market runs on **unmoderated WhatsApp messages.** No verification,
no reviews, no receipts, no way to complain.

Parents who pay for tuition are buying **trust**, not just teaching. They want
to know who is teaching their child, in their home, at night.

> **We do not need to solve this. But we must not make it worse.** If we build
> anything involving real people and minors, verification and parent visibility
> have to be there from day one, not added later.

### Money is tight, and getting tighter

The 2022 economic crisis pushed families out of tuition. A Batticaloa tutor lost
a quarter of his students. One family dropped their A-Level daughter because
they could not afford it.

LKR 2,000 a month is a lot when your whole education budget is LKR 2,400.

> **Being cheap is a feature, not a sacrifice of quality.**

### Demand is seasonal, and very sharp

Tuition demand explodes a few months before the exam, then collapses after
results. Tutors have to survive a whole year on a few months of income.

**Grade 6 is much flatter than O/L.** Grade 6 has small term-test bumps but no
enormous spike. For a part-time teacher, Grade 6 is steadier income.

### The government wants tuition to shrink

There is policy work underway on classroom-based assessment, with the stated
hope of reducing the tuition industry. If part of the exam becomes classroom
marks, then "here is your school grade, explained" becomes useful.

**Unverified, but worth watching.**

## What this means for MathsGuru

### The realistic opportunity

We are **not** going to replace tuition. Tuition is trusted, human, expensive,
and works. A website cannot and should not try to beat a good teacher in a
room.

What we can do:

1. **Be free.** We cost nothing against LKR 2,000/month. That alone puts us in
   every home.
2. **Be the practice.** Tuition is 1-2 hours a week. Practice is what the other
   160 hours are for.
3. **Explain patiently.** A tired tutor has 20 students. We can explain the
   same thing 20 times without getting annoyed.
4. **Track progress.** Nobody else does this. A parent seeing real evidence is
   worth a lot.
5. **Work on a phone, cheaply, in Sinhala and Tamil.**

### What we should avoid

- Competing on being "better than tuition"
- Charging money early
- Anything that needs a laptop or fast internet
- English-only content
- Building features that only work for the student, ignoring the parent

---

## What we do not know

- **No reliable tutor hourly rate data exists for Sri Lanka.** Everything above
  is asking prices from adverts, which are usually higher than what people
  actually agree to pay.
- **No data on how families find tutors.** Our channel ranking is a guess.
- **No data on how many tuition students exist.** The government figure counts
  households, not students.
- **Colombo versus rural rates are our estimate**, not measured. This is the
  number we most want to be wrong about.
