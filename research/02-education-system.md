# How Education Works in Sri Lanka

**Research date: 9 October 2026**

You cannot build for Sri Lankan students without knowing how their school day
and exam year actually works. This document covers the structure.

---

## The school system

Sri Lanka has one of the more literate and highly educated populations in South
Asia. Education has been free since the 1945 **Kannangara reforms**, and school
attendance is very high.

But the exam system is fierce.

### The stages

| Stage | Years | Age | Ends with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary** | Years 1-5 | 5-10 | Nothing |
| **Secondary** | Years 6-11 | 11-16 | **O-Level exam** |
| **Upper secondary** | Years 12-13 | 16-18 | **A-Level exam** |

There are about **3.6 million students** in Grades 1-11, and about **421,000**
in Grades 12-13.

### The naming trap - and a correction

**Sri Lanka calls it Grade 6. So do Sinhala and Tamil speakers.**

- Sinhala: **6 ශ්‍රේණිය** (sixth year)
- Tamil: **ஆறாம் வகுப்பு** (sixth class)

The National Education Commission has explicitly corrected this misconception:

> *"it uses **"Grades" as opposed to "Years"**... because the kindergarten year
> in Sri Lanka is designated as Grade 1... **This has often created a
> misconception.**"*

"Year 7" is the **UK equivalent** used in international comparisons. It is not a
Sri Lankan name, and **"Year 6" in Sinhala or Tamil means Grade 5**.

**Our earlier research claimed Grade 6 was officially called Year 7. That was
wrong.** Use "Grade 6" - it is correct in every language.

## Medium of instruction

A child's **medium** is chosen early and usually stays fixed for life.

### How many schools

| Medium | Number of schools |
| --- | --- |
| Sinhala only | **6,357** (62.6% of schools, **73.3% of students**) |
| Tamil only | **3,042** (30.0% of schools, **24.3% of students**) |
| Sinhala + English bilingual | 524 |
| Tamil + English bilingual | 157 |
| Trilingual | 33 |

From the **Annual School Census 2020**, via the NEC National Education Policy
Framework. Note the gap between schools and students: Tamil schools are on
average smaller, so Tamil is **30% of schools but 24% of students**.

> **Correction to our earlier research:** we wrote that Sinhala and Tamil medium
> students sit the English Maths paper. **They do not.** The Department of
> Examinations publishes every O-Level paper in Sinhala, Tamil and English, and
> students sit the exam in their own medium.

### Why this matters more than it looks

Mathematics is the odd one out:

> **The government has actively pushed to teach Mathematics in English from
> Grade 6 onwards** (bilingual programme, Circular 2001/05, and repeated NEC
> recommendations).

So a Sinhala or Tamil medium student is often:

- **Reading** maths in English
- **Thinking** in Sinhala or Tamil

They follow the symbols but cannot ask the question in the language they think
in. And per World Bank data, only about **10%** of students reach target English
proficiency, with English writing skills close to zero outside cities.

### The constitutional position

- Sinhala or Tamil is the **medium of instruction**, protected by the
  Constitution.
- Tamil is the medium in the **north and east**.
- English is the official **link language**.
- Current policy direction is **trilingual**, and students should be able to sit
  O/L and A/L **in the medium of their choice**.

### What this means for us

| Segment | Share | Is English a barrier? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grades 1-5, Sinhala/Tamil | Large | **Severely** |
| Grades 6-11, Sinhala medium | Large | Partial - think in Sinhala, read English |
| Grades 6-11, Tamil medium | ~25% of schools | Same, in Tamil |
| English medium | ~1.4% | No |
| A/L science | Smaller | No - university is English |

> **"English only" would lock out roughly three quarters of the school system.
> And a Sinhala explanation of a concept the student understands is worth more
> than an English one they cannot follow.**

## The two national exams

### O-Level (Ordinary Level)

The exam at the end of Grade 11. It decides:

- Which stream you enter at A-Level
- Which university courses you can apply for
- Whether a diploma, apprenticeship or job is realistic

**It is the single most important exam in a Sri Lankan student's life.**

Thousands of students sit it in the same few days, across the whole country.
The scale is part of why it is stressful.

### A-Level (Advanced Level)

Taken in Grades 12-13, after O-Level. Two years. Students choose a stream:
Pure Mathematics, Applied Mathematics, Combined Mathematics, or a
non-mathematics combination.

Needed for university, especially in science and engineering.

### Exam dates move. Always check.

**O/L is normally held in December**, and December is being restored.

But the last few years were badly disrupted by COVID and the economic crisis,
so the date moved several times:

| Exam year | When it was held | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2019 | December 2019 | Normal |
| 2020 | March 2021 | COVID-19 |
| 2022-2023 | Postponed several times | Economic crisis |
| 2024 | March 2025 | Still recovering |
| 2025 | February 2026 | Still recovering |
| **2026** | **8-17 December 2026** | **Back to normal** |
| 2027 | 7-17 December 2027 | Confirmed |

> **Never hard-code the exam month into a study plan.** Count back from the
> published date instead. The official calendar is at
> [doenets.lk](https://www.doenets.lk/examcalendar).

**Worth noting:** A-Level has moved the other way, from November-December to
August-September. The two exams no longer run in the same order they used to.

### Results

Results come about 3-4 months after the exam. A December exam means results in
March or April.

| Exam | Results |
| --- | --- |
| 2024 exam (March 2025) | 11 July 2025 |
| 2025 exam (February 2026) | 19 June 2026 |

A restored December 2026 exam points to results around March 2027, but **no date
has been announced**.

### The academic year

The school year runs roughly **January to December**, with **two or three terms**
and term tests in between. School usually starts in January.

Term tests matter more than students expect. They are the only feedback before
the O-Level, and parents see them.

## The results: why Z-scores confuse everyone

### Raw marks do not compare across years

Exam difficulty changes. A 55% one year might be a 48% the next. So raw marks
are meaningless on their own.

### Enter the Z-score

A **Z-score** normalises your result against everyone else in your **district**.

- Better district averages produce higher Z-scores for the same raw mark
- Your Z-score is what universities use
- A student with a high mark and a low Z-score may not get the course they want

**Students worry about Z-scores more than pass marks.** This is genuinely
confusing to anyone from another country, and worth explaining on our site.

### Grades

Results come out as letter grades with grade points. Students chase specific
grades, not just passes.

**The grades students care about are decided mostly by district competition.**
This is why tuition is everywhere.

## School types

### Government schools

The majority of students. Free. Exam results vary enormously between schools
and districts.

Many government school teachers tutor on the side for extra income. A study of
Anuradhapura found nearly half of teachers use leave entitlement to teach
tuition.

### Private schools

Fee-paying. Generally better resourced. Still follow the same national syllabus
and sit the same exams.

### International schools

Cambridge or Edexcel syllabus, English instruction. Different exams, higher
fees, and **much higher tutor rates** - online international O/L tutoring runs
around LKR 2,500-3,000 per hour.

## The reality of the school day

For a student in Grades 6-11:

- School runs roughly **7:30am to 1:30pm**
- Most students then go to **tuition**, often for one or more subjects
- Homework in the evening
- **Students miss more school as O-Level approaches**, because tuition takes
  priority

For a Grade 6 student, the realistic picture is:

```
Wake 5:00  ->  School 7:30-13:30  ->  Tuition 14:00-17:00
            ->  Homework 17:00-20:00  ->  Sleep
```

### What this means for us

There is no large window of free evening time. A student who spends three hours
in tuition classes has very little left.

> **Our product has to fit into the small gaps. Ten minutes, not an hour.**

This is consistent with the research finding that **under 10 minutes a week of
usage produces no measurable effect**. So the honest goal is: make ten minutes
count, and get the student to do it daily.

## Key facts to remember

| Fact | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| It is "Grade 6", in every language | "Year 7" is a UK mapping. Avoid it |
| O-Level decides everything | It drives all student behaviour |
| O-Level Maths = one subject, 200 marks | Not Pure/Applied/Combined. Those are A-Level |
| Sinhala is 73% of students, Tamil 24% | Only ~1% of online maths content is Tamil |
| Exam is sat in the student's own medium | Not always English |
| Z-scores are district-relative | Explains tuition culture |
| Exam dates move | Never hard-code them |
| School ends ~1:30pm | Small gaps only |
| ~3.6 million students in Grades 1-11 | The scale of the opportunity |
