# The O-Level Mathematics Syllabus

**Sources: NIE Grade 11 Mathematics Teacher's Guide (2016), and the actual
Department of Examinations 2025 exam paper**

---

## First, a correction

Earlier drafts of this research described the O-Level syllabus as having
**Pure, Applied and Combined Mathematics** branches.

**That is wrong.** Those are A-Level subjects.

> **At O-Level there is exactly ONE Mathematics subject, code 32.**

Verified against the Department of Examinations' own 2025 paper set. The
subject list contains a single "Mathematics" entry (code 32).

| Level | Maths subjects |
| --- | --- |
| **O-Level** | **Mathematics only (32)** |
| **A-Level** | Mathematics (07), **Combined Mathematics** (10), Higher Mathematics (11) |

**Why this matters:** we build toward **one** syllabus of 25 units. The
Pure/Applied/Combined split only matters if we extend to Grades 12-13, which is
not the current plan.

---

## The syllabus structure

Grade 11 uses the same competency architecture as Grade 6, with added domain
headings grouping competencies into Numbers, Algebra, Geometry, Measurement,
Relations, Data and Probability.

**25 units, 190 periods, three terms.**

It opens with 8 National Goals and 7 National Competencies, aligned to the
National Learning Competency Framework.

## The exam, verified from the actual 2025 paper

From the Department of Examinations' 2025 (2026) O-Level Mathematics paper:

### Two papers, 200 marks total

| | Paper I | Paper II |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Time** | **2 hours** | **3 hours + 10 min reading** |
| **Pages** | 8 | 4 |
| **Part A** | Q1-25, **MCQ, 2 marks each = 50** | Q1-6, answer 5, 10 marks each = 50 |
| **Part B** | Q1-5, structured, **10 marks each = 50** | Q7-12, answer 5, 10 marks each = 50 |
| **Total** | **100** | **100** |

**Grand total: 200 marks.** Confirmed by the official 2020 marking scheme.

### Three things most people get wrong

**1. MCQs are worth 2 marks, not 1.** So 25 MCQs = 50 marks. Students who
assume 1 mark each misjudge the weighting by 25 marks.

**2. Paper I is not all multiple choice.** Part B is 5 extended questions with
sub-parts (i) to (iv), worth 10 marks each. Students who revise only for MCQ
are preparing for half the paper.

> **Earlier drafts of our research said Paper I was "25 short questions and 5
> structured". That was right, but we did not flag that students routinely
> assume the whole paper is MCQ. It is a genuine and costly misconception.**

**3. Paper II is entirely non-MCQ.** You answer 10 questions: 5 from Part A and
5 from Part B. Part A is application-style. Part B is proof, geometry, and
statistics, including "show that" questions.

### Instructions from the paper

> *"Answer **ten** questions selecting **five** questions from **Part A** and
> **five** questions from **Part B**... Each question carries 10 marks."*

The paper also prints standard formulae:

> *"The volume of a right prism of cross sectional area A and height h is Ah.
> The volume of a sphere of radius r is 4/3 pi r^3."*

And the standard preamble on both papers: **take pi as 22/7.**

## School-Based Assessment (SBA)

From MoE Circular 23/2017:

- **Grades 6-9:** one assessment stage per term per subject, compulsory
- **O/L:** five stages count - Terms 1-3 of Grade 10, Terms 1-2 of Grade 11
- **A/L:** five stages plus a Team Project

### The important exception

> *"If a simple pass has been obtained in written test in Ordinary Level
> Examination for a subject and a credit or higher competency level has been
> obtained for that subject under the School Based Assessment Program, it will
> be considered as one credit pass. **But this is not relevant to medium
> language and mathematics.**"*

**SBA cannot substitute for the O-Level Mathematics written result.** Maths is
explicitly excluded.

> **Consequence:** we should not build SBA-alternative features for maths. The
> written exam is the only thing that counts.

## New and old syllabus

**The O-Level maths syllabus changed for papers from 2017 onward.**

The rationalized Grade 10-11 syllabus (introduced 2015) reached the exam in 2017.
Papers before that test the old syllabus.

| Year | Syllabus |
| --- | --- |
| 2015 and earlier | **Old** |
| 2016-2017 | Transition - DoE publishes **both** |
| 2017 onward | **New (current)** |

> **Every practice question must be labelled with its syllabus vintage.** A 2014
> paper and a 2024 paper are not the same course. This is easy to get wrong and
> produces practice material that does not match the exam.

## What this means for the product

### Scope discipline

One subject, 25 units, 190 periods. That is the entire O-Level target.

**This is bigger than Grade 6** (25 units, 157 periods) but the unit count is the
same. The difficulty is much higher: proofs, trigonometry, matrices.

### Start at Grade 6, not here

The research says build one topic properly. Grade 6 is 157 periods of content
at a level where quality is achievable.

O-Level is where the stakes are highest, and where every competitor already
operates.

> **Grade 6 first is not a compromise. It is the same 25-unit shape at a level
> where we can actually do it well.**

### Design implications from the exam structure

| Exam feature | What it means for us |
| --- | --- |
| 200 marks total | Track the real weighting, not a made-up score |
| MCQ worth 2 marks | Our practice must reflect this |
| Paper I Part B is structured | Not everything is multiple choice |
| Paper II: answer 10 of 12 | **Time management is a trainable skill** |
| 10 min reading time | Part of the skill set |
| Show-that questions | Proof needs its own teaching mode |
| Formulas printed on paper | Students get these free. Teach the selection, not the recall |

**The "answer 10 of 12" structure is a gift for us.** It means time management
is not incidental - it is part of the syllabus. A student who cannot pace
themselves will lose marks even knowing everything. That is a teachable,
practisable skill, and nobody in Sri Lanka teaches it.
